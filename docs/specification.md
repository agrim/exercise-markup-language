# Exercise Markup Language 1.0

An exercise is a UTF-8 JSON document describing one timed cycle or held pose. A consumer can resolve it without a catalog lookup. `id` names a document for discovery and reuse. Changing only the ID or title must produce identical movement. The normative structural contract is [exercise-v1.schema.json](../schema/exercise-v1.schema.json). This document supplies the interpretation and cross-reference checks that JSON Schema cannot express.

## Body, time and equipment coordinates

The reference humanoid faces +Z, with Y up and +X toward **its** left. Distances are metres, time is seconds, angles are degrees. Its root is the pelvis. Joint translations in the anatomy file are bind positions in world space, with identity bind rotations. Author `root` as a displacement from that bind pose. Joint angles are local active rotations about X, then Y, then Z (on a vector the X rotation acts first). The compiled form uses local unit quaternions in XYZW order. Named joints, muscle groups and measured palm, grip, heel and forefoot landmarks are in the [rig](../anatomy/model.json). A camera is a presentation hint, separate from the body's orientation.

Equipment is instantiated once per exercise. Each instance has a unique local `id` and a registered `kind`. Three dumbbells require three instances. The [equipment registry](../library/equipment.json) supplies local sockets, dimensions and illustrative geometry. A world-driven item takes a world position and rotation. A joint-driven item takes a `follow.joint`, local offset and local rotation. The joint's solved world transform drives its position and orientation. A socket override changes a registered point in the item's local frame, e.g. the distance between grips on the same bar. Geometry components are illustrative. Actual gym apparatus dimensions vary.

A `connection` links two item sockets for a cable, rope, band or rigid link. Its visual segment follows the two endpoints. It describes the line of pull, without calculating load, tension or slack. A `support` records which body segment bears on the floor or an item. A hard contact names an effector the solver must place. These two fields distinguish teaching cues such as “upper back against the bench” from measured end-effector targets.

## Levels of detail

| Level | What the author supplies | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting posture | `start.pose`, optional `stance`, optional `parameters` | Inherit a registered or document-local pose. A stance sets heel separation and outward toe angle, seats the feet on the floor and lowers the root as needed for reach. |
| Coordinated motion | Each phase lists `actions` by name and amplitude | A macro contributes deltas to several joint channels and/or the root. Macros can compose other macros. An amount of 0 uses none of the delta and 0.5 uses half. |
| Local detail | `start.joints` or `phase.detail.joints`, with a triple or selected axes | An explicitly supplied axis replaces that authored angle. Unspecified axes and joints retain the macro result. A phase root override specifies the absolute bind-relative translation. |
| Contacts | Measured effector, target, optional pole and world orientation | Inverse kinematics adjusts the relevant two-link limb to reach the required target. The compiler reports an unsatisfied request. Joint detail on a constrained limb guides the bend but cannot force contact to slide. |

A stance width may be a number in metres, `"shoulder-width"`, `"hip-width"`, or `{"of":"shoulder-width","factor":1.15}`. The profile measures left-to-right upper arm or thigh joint centers across the X axis in bind pose. Toe-out can be given in degrees or as `forward` (0°), `slight-out` (12°), or `out` (25°).

Posture inheritance copies a parent's pose, then replaces each authored child channel. A child `root` replaces its parent's root. A new pose can be composed of joint detail alone. Motion deltas add to the starting pose. A motion's nested actions sum first, followed by its own local deltas; repeating an action is additive. Evaluating a macro produces the same deltas regardless of the exercise ID. A motion's `root` is a **delta**; a pose or detail root is an **absolute displacement**. Within a single joint map, bilateral shorthand such as `bothThigh` expands first; right-side Y and Z signs reflect across the body's midline. An explicit left or right joint then replaces only its specified axes.

The file may define additional poses and motions in `definitions`. Names cannot shadow registered symbols. Unknown symbols, cycles and nesting deeper than 16 definitions are errors. Parameters have finite minimum, maximum and default values. Use `{"param":"depth"}` in a numeric socket, then supply overrides when constructing the program. Validate both the declared range and the receiving field's range. Clients should preserve explicitly chosen values when saving a variant.

## Phases, contacts and repetitions

Each phase describes an **endpoint** and duration. Its endpoint starts from the exercise's original setup, applies actions, then applies detail. An empty action list returns to setup unless detail changes an endpoint. To pause at the bottom of a squat, repeat the bottom endpoint in a timed phase. Omitted item fields likewise resolve against the original item, not the previous endpoint. The player interpolates *from the preceding endpoint* to this endpoint. Joint Euler values interpolate as authored, without wrapping across ±180°. Split a long swing into intermediate phases when its path matters. Equipment world positions and driver offsets interpolate linearly in the chosen ease. A phase may set an item’s `controlPosition` for a quadratic Bézier path between world positions. The control applies only to that interval, and cannot be combined with a joint driver. Default `smooth` uses 3u²−2u³; `linear` uses u; `ease-in` and `ease-out` use 1−cos(πu/2) and sin(πu/2). The endpoint at an exact boundary belongs to the phase just completed.

Base contacts apply in every phase unless that phase supplies a complete replacement list. An empty replacement releases all contacts, including stance pins. Inherited stance heel contacts are combined with explicit base contacts; the explicit target for that landmark wins. `start` targets the landmark's setup position, captured after stance fitting and before other constraints. `floor` projects that anchor onto Y=0. A point target is in the world. An item/socket target follows the rigid item's current transform. A pole is a world-space bend direction. An optional contact rotation prescribes the hand or foot's world orientation. An equipment grip uses the anatomy profile's closed fingers unless individual finger channels are authored. A hand-driven implement can instead specify `follow.grip:true` to close the driving hand.

An optional `rootAnchor` translates the body so a named joint stays at its setup world position (`target:"start"`) or an explicit world point. A kneeling rollout can keep the knee pivot fixed between endpoints while the torso rotates. Apply this translation after interpolating authored angles and before solving limb contacts. Report a conflicting root anchor if the limb solve subsequently displaces it beyond tolerance. A phase may replace it or release it with `rootAnchor:null`.

Default engagement is `pin`: the target applies throughout that phase. Use `engage:"reach"` to move smoothly from the landmark's preceding solved endpoint to a target rather than teleport on acquisition. For moving equipment, aim at its current interpolated socket. Prefer world-driven or independent body drivers when reaching onto a moving object. Pole directions and compatible foot orientations should agree if both the heel and ball of one sole have targets. A driven object and its gripping limbs cannot depend cyclically on one another. Unreachable targets produce diagnostics with residuals in metres. The reference contact tolerance is 0.012 m. A failed check must not silently turn into a freely moving hand or foot.

`repeat.mode` is `once`, `loop`, or `hold`. `count` is the number of cycles in the finite demonstration. A `once` has count 1 and can finish in a new pose. A `hold` retains its pose for the sum of phase durations per cycle. A `loop` returns the body and apparatus to the next cycle's start. Optional `repeat.travel` offsets the next root and body-relative setup anchors by a displacement per cycle; fixed world points and equipment stay in their world frames. The closing pose must match that translated start. At the last repetition, playback holds the final pose. A producer can request repetitions separately from workout-tracking logic.

## Muscle display

`muscles.primary` and `secondary` contain distinct anatomy group IDs. All other groups are unhighlighted. Per-phase `muscles` replaces the exercise roles for that phase. Optional `sideFocus` restricts named active groups to the anatomical side in sorted, non-overlapping half-open intervals of normalized cycle time; both sides show outside intervals. It wraps at the loop seam. Muscle roles are instructional annotations. A neutral color does not establish absence of physiological activity. The reference model has 28 highlightable regions and does not quantify activation.

## Conformance and evolution

A reader must reject unknown core fields, versions and referenced vocabulary rather than guessing. A strict producer accepts duplicate-free JSON, finite numbers and the published bounds on counts, durations and amplitudes. Static validity, reference resolution, and solved motion are distinct tests. The reference checker samples phase interiors, compares both sides of phase transitions, detects slipping or impossible grips and checks hold/loop boundaries. A shared test report gives reproducible sampling evidence, not a proof of collision-free motion at every instant. Educational supports and free-body balance are outside the hard-contact solver.

The portable compiled frame contains time in seconds, phase ID, root translation, local joint quaternions, world joint positions and measured landmarks, item transforms and derived sockets, effective muscle roles/supports, and diagnostics. Quaternions q and −q are equivalent. The compiled JSON contains one cycle, with repeat policy supplied separately. Native or other renderers can interpolate and cache this result. Readers that implement only a profile must explicitly reject features they cannot represent.

A 1.0 reader accepts `eml:"1.0"`. New catalog entries and registry additions can extend v1 without changing the meaning of existing programs. A change to the interpretation of a socket, axis, existing primitive, or default requires compatibility review, examples and a versioned migration. The schema, vocabulary, tests and public specification together form the interface.
